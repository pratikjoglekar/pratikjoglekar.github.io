6. DECISION META-LAYER
===============================================================
 
WHEN MAKING A DECISION I EVALUATE:
 
- Reversibility: Can this be safely rolled back?
- Governance impact: Does this introduce structural debt or unclear ownership?
- Economic alignment: Does this compound long-term value?
- Behavioral clarity: Does this reduce confusion or add it?
- Operational cost: Does this shift burden to support, ops, or future teams?
 
WHEN I CHOOSE SPEED OVER CORRECTNESS:
 
- The decision is reversible.
- Structural risk is low.
- Learning velocity outweighs system risk (e.g., Coursera AI-assisted prototyping under GTM pressure).
 
WHEN I CHOOSE CORRECTNESS OVER SPEED:
 
- Governance, ownership, or pricing models are impacted.
- Economic integrity is at risk (e.g., HubSpot retention framed as dollar retention, not raw churn).
- Compounding fragility would result downstream (e.g., not replacing analytics tools wholesale at HubSpot).

PROJECT 1: Coursera for Campus — Native LTI Integrations
===============================================================
 
ROLE: Sole Product Designer
TEAM: 1 Designer, 8 Developers, 1 PM, 1 Data Analyst
DURATION: 8 months and ongoing (started July 2025)
DISCIPLINES: UXR, Design Strategy, System Design, UX, UI
 
SCALE & CONTEXT:
- Enterprise platform serving institutional LMS integrations across ~13,000 enterprise accounts.
- Replaces SchoolDay (GG4L) third-party middleware with a first-party LTI capability inside Canvas, Blackboard, and Moodle.
 
PROBLEM:
- 10% successful LMS connection rate.
- Middleware dependency limited reliability, observability, and product velocity.
- 57% of enterprise admin issues escalated to support.
 
FRAMING SHIFT:
- Initial framing: "ship a working LMS integration."
- Reframed as: "treat integrations as a first-class, first-party platform capability."
 
TRIGGER MOMENTS:
- Sync failures (grade passback, activity sync) were impossible to diagnose because Coursera had no observability into middleware.
- Faculty workflows did not map cleanly to LMS curricula, forcing manual workarounds.
- Setup overhead was blocking enterprise GTM motion.
 
KEY DECISIONS:
 
  Decision: Build native LTI rather than continue institution-specific custom setups.
  Why: Custom integrations created operational overhead and capped scalability.
  Tradeoff: Less flexibility for edge-case LMS configurations.
  Mitigation: Broad LTI standards support + bounded configuration for institutional needs.
 
  Decision: Real-time grade and enrollment sync rather than periodic batch.
  Why: Delayed records eroded instructor and admin trust.
  Tradeoff: Higher technical complexity and dependency on LMS reliability.
  Mitigation: Clear error states, retries, transparent sync status.
 
  Decision: Opinionated, LTI-standards-aligned flow over exposing all configuration upfront.
  Why: Most institutions share core needs; over-flexibility increased error rates and support load.
  Tradeoff: Power users had fewer customization options.
  Mitigation: Progressive disclosure of advanced settings; documented supported extensions.
 
METHODS USED:
- AI-assisted prototyping for rapid end-to-end concept under GTM time pressure.
- Focused group with CSMs, Implementation Managers, and Solutions Architects to surface frontline pain.
- MVP usability testing.
- Deep-dive customer interviews on personalization, AI, search/browse, and decision support.
- Cross-functional workshop to prioritize against user impact, feasibility, and platform leverage.
 
OUTCOMES:
- LMS connection success: 10% → 23%.
- Support dependency: 57% → 7% over 2 quarters.
- Pricing/monetization redesign: churn -37%, checkout completion +11%.
 
ORGANIZATIONAL IMPACT:
- Repositioned integrations as durable, owned infrastructure rather than an external add-on.
- Reduced third-party dependency and increased velocity of future integration work.
 
OPEN QUESTIONS / NEXT:
- Final visual design is in flight.
- Continued expansion of LMS coverage and observability.

PROJECT 2: Usage Intelligence Platform — HubSpot
===============================================================
 
ROLE: Sole Product Designer
TEAM: 1 Designer, 4 Developers, 1 PM, 1 Data Analyst
DURATION: 5 months
DISCIPLINES: UXR, Design Strategy, System Design, UX, UI
 
SCALE & CONTEXT:
- Internal usage intelligence and governance platform for HubSpot's SMB customers.
- Cross-functional with Product, Engineering, and Data.
- Affects reporting integrity and downstream decision reliability across product and customer teams.
 
PROBLEM:
- 8.5% expected critical usage gap rate.
- Fragmented event ownership; event logic lived in code files with unclear accountability.
- Reactive incident handling — failures often surfaced only after propagating to external analytics tools.
- Existing exploration tools optimized for analysis, not governance or action.
 
FRAMING SHIFT:
- Initial framing: "improve analytics visibility."
- Reframed as: "establish event ownership and governance — a complementary layer, not a replacement for analytics tools."
 
TRIGGER MOMENTS:
- Multiple anomaly incidents had no accountable owner.
- Deleting a code file silently deleted live event data with no safeguard or signal.
- Failures persisted despite the existence of exploration tooling.
 
KEY DECISIONS:
 
  Decision: Reject building another analytics surface; build governance and ownership instead.
  Why: Existing tools optimized for exploration, not accountability.
  Tradeoff: Less flexibility for ad-hoc analysis.
  Mitigation: Position as complementary; coexist with Amplitude/Looker.
 
  Decision: Flexible event schema over exhaustive upfront standardization.
  Why: Teams needed to move fast; heavy governance would block adoption.
  Tradeoff: Risk of inconsistency early on.
  Mitigation: Validation, auditing, and alerting as safety nets.
 
METHODS USED:
- 8 SMB customer interviews on data experience and gaps.
- Cross-functional workshop to map current system, surface technical constraints, and align on direction.
- Stakeholder alignment on ownership model and decision principles.
 
OUTCOMES:
- Critical usage gap reduced 8.5% → 5.1% (40% improvement, exceeding target).
- Event-tracking data reliability +64% (anomaly detection system).
- Critical data errors -70% (event creation flow simplification).
- No-code event creation enabling faster instrumentation.
- Audit trails and ownership tracking that persisted beyond launch.
 
ORGANIZATIONAL IMPACT:
- Shifted teams from reactive investigation to proactive monitoring.
- Introduced shared event ownership and audit trails.
- Governance persisted beyond the initial rollout.
 
COUNTERFACTUAL:
- If governance had been ignored: event inconsistencies would have scaled, support burden would have grown, and trust in analytics would have eroded.

PROJECT 3: Retention Intervention at Cancellation — HubSpot
===============================================================
 
ROLE: Sole Product Designer
TEAM: 1 Designer, 2 Developers, 1 PM, 1 Data Analyst
DURATION: 2 months
DISCIPLINES: UXR, Design Strategy, Data Analysis, UX, UI
 
SCALE & CONTEXT:
- SaaS subscription product with early churn breaking starter unit economics.
- CAC payback period was approaching ~2 years; customers churning before payback created negative unit economics.
 
PROBLEM:
- Churn was framed behaviorally rather than economically.
- Starter customers were leaving before reaching CAC payback.
- Risk of permanent pricing changes if handled bluntly.
 
FRAMING SHIFT:
- Reframed from "reduce churn" to "improve dollar retention and lifetime value."
- Anchored success on dollar retention and post-discount continuation, not cancellation deferral.
 
KEY DECISIONS:
 
  Decision: Frame around unit economics and LTV, not raw churn reduction.
  Why: Churn alone masked the true business risk — leaving before CAC payback.
  Tradeoff: Some retained users would still churn later, hurting short-term churn optics.
  Mitigation: Measure success via dollar retention and post-discount continuation.
 
  Decision: Time-bound discount only at cancellation, not earlier in the journey.
  Why: Cancellation is the moment of explicit intent and maximum price sensitivity.
  Tradeoff: No reach for users who churn silently.
  Mitigation: Tightly scoped experiment to validate impact before broadening signals.
 
  Decision: Constrain experiment to a narrowly defined, high-risk starter cohort (Starter Customer Platform, seats-based, monthly commit / monthly pay).
  Why: Reduce noise, increase causal confidence.
  Tradeoff: Results don't immediately generalize.
  Mitigation: Treat as validation; define expansion criteria upfront.
 
METHODS USED:
- Competitive analysis across VEED.IO, Jasper.ai, Notion, Intercom, ClickFunnels, Canva.
- Cohort analysis with data analyst.
- End-to-end cancellation flow mapping to identify the right intervention moment.
- Mini-checkout with immediate discount visibility and clear legal language.
 
DOWNSIDE RISK & INVALIDATION:
- Risk of conditioning price sensitivity.
- Risk of short-term revenue distortion.
- Invalidation criteria: post-discount continuation below 5%, or LTV failing to exceed CAC.
 
OUTCOMES:
- 35.8% dollar retention among discount recipients.
- 14% post-discount continuation (sustained product usage beyond the discount window).
- Repeatable, cohort-based retention experimentation model.
 
ORGANIZATIONAL IMPACT:
- Shifted retention framing toward economics rather than promotion.
- Established a governed retention experimentation model that could be applied to similar segments.
 
WHAT I'D DO DIFFERENTLY:
- Systematically A/B test discount levels. The 50% rate validated the hypothesis but optimal threshold was not isolated.

PROJECT 4: Dashboard Redesign — MassMutual
===============================================================
 
ROLE: Lead Product Designer (sole designer on this surface)
TEAM: 1 Designer, 7 Developers, 1 Product Owner, 1 Business Analyst, 1 Data Analyst
DURATION: 6 months
DISCIPLINES: UXR, Design Strategy, Data Analysis, UX, UI
 
SCALE & CONTEXT:
- Insurance platform homepage serving as the primary entry point for life insurance and retirement product policyholders.
- Customer portfolios growing in complexity (multiple products, coverage types, beneficiaries).
 
PROBLEM:
- Navigation-heavy homepage; outdated design system limiting scannability.
- Engagement time: 15.02 seconds.
- Mental model confusion across policy data.
- Information depth was insufficient; "face value" wasn't the metric customers cared most about.
 
FRAMING SHIFT:
- Reframed from "navigational dashboard" to "decision-making control surface."
 
TRIGGER MOMENTS:
- 9 of 10 customers found quick links unhelpful — they introduced steps rather than removing them.
- 8 of 10 wanted to learn about their policies directly from the homepage.
- 9 of 10 wanted payment context alongside richer policy details.
 
KEY DECISIONS:
 
  Decision: Treat the homepage as a decision-making control surface, not a navigational dashboard.
  Why: Research showed users logged in to complete financial and policy-critical tasks, not to browse.
  Tradeoff: Reduced emphasis on secondary features and quick links.
  Mitigation: Support deeper exploration contextually.
 
  Decision: Full redesign rather than incremental visual fixes.
  Why: The existing information model could not support growing policy complexity.
  Tradeoff: Higher upfront cost and longer delivery timeline.
  Mitigation: Anchor the work in reusable patterns and scalable components.
 
  Decision: Select Northstar Design B over Design A.
  Why: Design B better guided users through complex policy info (ease-of-use 7.85/8 vs 6.6/8).
  Tradeoff: Required hierarchy tuning to improve discoverability of key actions.
  Mitigation: Refined layout, placement, emphasis based on usability feedback.
 
METHODS USED:
- Quantitative VOC survey with 4.7k customers — 75% Billing & payments, 50% Policy info, 50% Dividends, 38% Beneficiaries, 25% Loans, 25% Other.
- Existing-design usability study.
- Competitive analysis (industry homepages were navigational dashboards; opportunity to differentiate).
- Northstar usability study comparing two design directions.
 
OUTCOMES:
- Engagement time: 15.02s → 34.66s (more than doubled).
- Resume framing: "user adoption +9%."
- Design patterns reused across additional product surfaces.
 
ORGANIZATIONAL IMPACT:
- Established a decision-oriented homepage philosophy.
- Created a scalable design language extended across policy, product, and transactional experiences.
- Influenced subsequent mobile design system work and term-policy experience.
